# Mock exams (M1–M4)

These sessions are for exam practice only; they produce no project work.

| Session | Scope | Format |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | Chapters 1–2 | 15 questions, untimed, explanation after each block of 5 |
| M2 | Chapters 3–4 | 20 questions, untimed, explanation after each block of 5 |
| M3 | Chapters 1–5 | Full mock, **timed**, explanations at the end. On the same day, also do the **official iSAQB mock exam** (download it from isaqb.org). |
| M4 | Chapters 1–5 | Second full mock, timed, weighted toward the weak spots in `notes/progress.md` |

## Rules for the sparring partner

1. Weight the questions by relevance: mostly **R1**, some **R2**, **no R3**. Take the learning goals from `reference/text/curriculum-foundation-en-2025.1-rev2.txt`; don't invent topics outside it.
2. Mix A-, P- and K-type questions (see `CLAUDE.md`). Use neutral business domains, not the robot.
3. Present all questions of a block first. Collect the user's answers. Then score and explain each wrong answer, quoting the learning goal (LG number and short title).
4. Before writing the questions, ask the user for the exact exam format they've been told (number of questions, duration, pass mark) and simulate that for M3 and M4. Don't assume figures.
5. At the end, append to `notes/progress.md`: the score per chapter, the LGs answered wrongly, and the top 3 topics to review.

- [ ] I know the design principles with examples, R1 ones first (LG 03-04).
- [ ] I can explain Layers, Pipes and Filters and Microservices, including their drawbacks (LG 03-08).
- [ ] I know the four views and what each contains (LG 04-05).
- [ ] I know the requirements for documentation (LG 04-01) and what an ADR contains (LG 04-08).
- [ ] I know the reasons for architecture analysis and the methods by quality (LG 05-01, 05-02).
